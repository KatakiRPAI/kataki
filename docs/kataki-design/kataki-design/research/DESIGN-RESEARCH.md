# Design research — Kataki RPAI visual direction

**Date:** 26 September 2026
**Method:** Live web research (search + source fetch) into current UI trends, dated/"AI slop" signals, and craft/art-led reference apps, gathered to inform the visual direction for Kataki's two registers — the bright, social **Sky** and the intimate, painterly **Scene**. Every claim below is tied to a real, fetched source, listed at the end. Where a source's exact wording is useful it is quoted; otherwise findings are paraphrased.

---

## 1. What is current

**Glassmorphism / "Liquid Glass"** — Apple's WWDC 2025 system-wide translucent, refractive material (frosted layers, "molten acrylic" glow) triggered a genuinely large and messy design event: months of user revolt over legibility, enough that Apple added a "Tinted" control in iOS 26.1 beta to tone it down ([BigGo](https://biggo.com/news/202510150133_Apple-Liquid-Glass-UI-Backlash), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/companies/apple-yields-tinted-control-in-ios-261-beta-4-tones-down-liquid-glass-after-backlash-1.500315176)). Commentary is split between "future of spatial computing" and "regressive, contrast-collapsing overcorrection for slowing AI progress" ([Webdesigner Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/how-apple-quietly-brought-skeuomorphism-back-to-life/), [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/apples-unrivalled-commitment-to-excellence-is-fading-a-designer-explains-why-274475)). Separately, a *refined* second-generation glassmorphism — lower saturation, tighter opacity control, paired with solid backgrounds rather than gradient blobs — is treated by working designers as legitimately useful again, but only on chrome (sidebars, modals, command bars) over photographic or illustrated content, not on data-dense surfaces ([rajeshrnair.com](https://rajeshrnair.com/blog/design/ui-ux/ui-design-trends-2026-bento-grids-glassmorphism.html)). **Verdict: peaking/contested at the OS level, cautiously ascending at the "refined" product level.** For Kataki: glass reads right as a *Scene* material (a pane of atmosphere between the reader and a painted world) but should never be the default surface for functional Sky chrome, where it will fight legibility and start to look like the exact backlash Apple is now walking back.

**Spatial and depth cues** — the same Liquid Glass moment pushed depth-through-translucency and parallax back into the mainstream vocabulary, explicitly framed as prep for spatial computing/AR glasses ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/13/apples-liquid-glass-design-is-paving-the-way-for-ar-glasses)). **Verdict: ascending, but speculative outside Apple's own platforms.** For Kataki: worth borrowing selectively — a Scene can use soft layered depth (background painting → mid weather/particle layer → foreground character → text) without needing literal glass.

**Bento layouts** — asymmetric, modular card grids remain one of the most cited 2026 patterns, praised for showing multiple features "without walls of text," but every source pairing it with a caution: it degrades into noise on data-dense dashboards or when forced where a uniform scan pattern is actually needed ([rajeshrnair.com](https://rajeshrnair.com/blog/design/ui-ux/ui-design-trends-2026-bento-grids-glassmorphism.html), [Mockuuups Studio](https://mockuuups.studio/blog/post/best-bento-grid-design-examples/)). **Verdict: ascending but easy to overuse.** Good candidate for the *Sky* browse/discovery layer (character cards of different sizes/importance); wrong for anything inside the Scene, which wants sequential reading, not a grid to scan.

**Expressive / variable typography** — described as an "underutilized practical improvement": variable fonts cut file weight 20–40% versus loading multiple static weights, and large, high-contrast display type is being used as a genuine brand/personality signal rather than decoration ([rajeshrnair.com](https://rajeshrnair.com/blog/design/ui-ux/ui-design-trends-2026-bento-grids-glassmorphism.html)). **Verdict: ascending, and cheap to do well.** Directly useful: Kataki can use one distinctive display face for Sky headers/character names and let body text stay quiet — the "single voice, aggressive negative tracking on display" approach Linear documents (see §4) is exactly this move.

**Material 3 Expressive** — Google's 2025 Android/Wear OS redesign, rolled out through late 2025, pushes bigger color, bouncier shape morphing, and motion-forward components, explicitly researched and defended by Google Design as a deliberate swing away from flat minimalism toward personality and emotional engagement ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/design-notes-material-3-expressive-liam-spradlin), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/11/17/google-material-3-expressive-redesign/)). **Verdict: ascending on Google's own platforms, not yet a cross-industry norm.** For Kataki: the *instinct* (warmth, shape, motion with personality) matches the brief, but Material 3's specific expressive shape-morphing language is a strong platform signature — better to take the philosophy than the componentry, or Kataki will read as "Android app," not as its own painted world.

**Motion and micro-interaction norms** — the consistent 2026 line across sources is that decorative motion is being actively rejected while *functional* motion (state changes, skeleton loading, progress, structure-explaining transitions) is rewarded: "the dividing line is clear: animations should communicate state or progress... decorative animations that work equally well removed should be cut" ([rajeshrnair.com](https://rajeshrnair.com/blog/design/ui-ux/ui-design-trends-2026-bento-grids-glassmorphism.html)); a near-identical framing appears independently as "Motion That Explains Structure," reframing animation as a clarity tool rather than a flourish ([Envato Elements](https://elements.envato.com/learn/ux-ui-design-trends)). **Verdict: this is the strongest, most repeated consensus point in the whole research.** For Kataki, this is a hard rule: every transition (Sky→Scene, message arriving, character portrait reacting) needs to *justify* itself as communicating a state change, not as ambient sparkle.

**Colour direction** — two things are true at once. Google's own products lean brighter and more saturated (Material 3 Expressive). Meanwhile the craft-reference tier (Linear, Raycast — §4) is trending *toward* near-monochrome, restrained accent color, with Linear explicitly having moved from a colored 2024 palette to "monochrome black/white with even fewer bold colors" in 2025 ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design/)). **Verdict: bifurcating — consumer/social products go saturated and expressive, tool/craft products go restrained.** Kataki's two-world structure maps onto this split cleanly: Sky can hold more saturation and warmth (it's the social, browsing layer), Scene should pull toward restraint so the painted art and the prose carry the color, not the chrome.

**Skeuomorphic revival** — real and citation-backed, but narrower than headlines suggest: Apple's Liquid Glass itself is described as bringing back tactile, material-simulating surfaces ("translucent layers, glowing refractions... molten acrylic") without literal texture skeuomorphism like stitched leather ([Webdesigner Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/how-apple-quietly-brought-skeuomorphism-back-to-life/)); the framing across sources is "skeuomorphic minimalism" — depth and materiality cues layered onto otherwise flat/modern structure, not a return to 2011-era textures. **Verdict: ascending in this narrow "material minimalism" sense, tired in its literal 2010 form.** Kataki should take tactile cues (paper grain, ink weight, a soft page-turn) as *texture applied to modern layout*, never full-blown UI skeuomorphism (fake wood, fake leather panels).

**Dark mode** — every current-trend source treats dark mode as table stakes, not a feature: "now table stakes for products targeting younger audiences, requiring proper palette design rather than mechanical color inversion" ([rajeshrnair.com](https://rajeshrnair.com/blog/design/ui-ux/ui-design-trends-2026-bento-grids-glassmorphism.html)); both Linear and Raycast (§4) are dark-only or dark-first by design-system default, using layered near-black surfaces rather than pure `#000000`. **Verdict: mandatory, not a trend.** Scene (evening reading, chat-at-night) almost certainly wants a considered dark palette as a first-class mode, built from a surface ladder rather than an inverted light theme.

## 2. What is worn out

- **The 2018 flat purple-to-blue gradient SaaS look.** This is now explicitly identified as *the* signature AI-generated-design tell (see §3) — its own long history (Stripe-era gradients → Tailwind's indigo/violet defaults → thousands of Series-A landing pages 2020–2023) is exactly why models regress to it ([uxskill.laithjunaidy.com](https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/blog/why-ai-uses-purple-gradients.html)).
- **Oversized hero blur blobs** — the soft abstract gradient-blob-behind-headline pattern is now a stock-asset category unto itself (thousands of "gradient blob" Figma/Framer components for sale), which is itself the tell that it's a template default rather than a considered choice, not an editorial one.
- **Generic Inter-everywhere** — Inter is treated in multiple slop-detection sources as *the* default-by-omission typeface, precisely because it is the safest, most averaged choice a model (or an unopinionated designer) can make; "Inter paired with a system fallback and nothing else" is called out directly as an authorship tell ([world.hey.com](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c), [925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)). Note: Inter itself isn't the problem (Raycast uses it deliberately, with a custom stylistic set — see §4); *undifferentiated* Inter with no typographic point of view is.
- **Neumorphism** — the soft-shadow, same-color-as-background "pressed foam" style had a famously fast rise-and-fall arc, openly mocked within the same year it appeared ("RIP neumorphism (2020–2020)") for failing contrast and accessibility ([Medium](https://medium.com/@monochrmstudios/rip-neumorphism-2020-2020-65d730cc9d67), [Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/neumorphism)). Thoroughly dead; no reason to revisit it.
- **Overuse of frosted/glass cards as a default surface**, rather than a deliberate material choice — this is the same critique running through both the Liquid Glass backlash and the AI-slop literature: glass used because it's *available*, not because it's earned ("glassmorphism / frosted panels" is a hard-banned pattern in one anti-slop design guide, [no-slop-ui](https://github.com/LeoStehlik/no-slop-ui)).
- **"Dashboard with four stat tiles"** — the reflexive metric-card grid is explicitly named as a default to avoid in the same anti-slop guide ("metric-card grid as the default dashboard layout"), alongside "hero sections inside internal dashboards" that don't need them.
- **Full-bleed stock-3D illustrations / generic abstract blobs** — flagged repeatedly as an AI-image tell (see §3); at the trend level, even optimistic sources note "widespread adoption has made default Midjourney aesthetics recognizable, potentially signaling lower effort" ([rajeshrnair.com](https://rajeshrnair.com/blog/design/ui-ux/ui-design-trends-2026-bento-grids-glassmorphism.html)).
- **Pill buttons everywhere with no hierarchy** — worth noting that the craft references *do* use pill shapes, but narrowly: Raycast reserves fully-rounded pills for exactly two roles (the primary CTA, and status/toggle pills) inside a border-radius scale that otherwise stays 4–16px ("the system never goes flat on cards and never above 16px except for fully-rounded pills") — the tell isn't the pill shape itself, it's using it for *every* button so nothing reads as more important than anything else.

## 3. The AI-slop checklist

Concrete, checkable tells drawn from active discussion of "AI design slop" — a term with its own growing literature ([no-slop-ui](https://github.com/LeoStehlik/no-slop-ui), [SmoothUI](https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop), [Spot the Slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c), [925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells), [uxskill.laithjunaidy.com](https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/blog/why-ai-uses-purple-gradients.html)).

| # | Tell (check for this) | How Kataki avoids it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | An indigo/violet-to-blue gradient anywhere in the palette, traceable to Tailwind's default accent — called "the single loudest AI tell in 2026" ([925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)) | Palette is drawn from Kataki's own painted references (Sky = warm daylight hues; Scene = per-story palette), never a framework default. No violet-blue accent anywhere. |
| 2 | Glassmorphism used as decoration rather than material, often "with a neon glow" ([SmoothUI](https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop)) | Glass is reserved for one purpose only (a pane over the Scene's painted world) and never used to dress up flat Sky chrome. |
| 3 | A glow effect under every card ([uxskill.laithjunaidy.com](https://uxskill.laithjunaidy.com/blog/why-ai-uses-purple-gradients.html)) | Depth comes from the surface-ladder approach Linear/Raycast use (one flat step lighter per elevation), not drop-shadow glow. |
| 4 | A row of three (or six) identical feature cards — same icon-heading-two-lines shape, same weight ([925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells), [SmoothUI](https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop)) | Sky uses an asymmetric bento arrangement where importance (a favorite character, an active story) is visually larger, not a uniform repeating unit. |
| 5 | Undifferentiated Inter (or any system default) with no stylistic choice — "the safest possible answer" ([925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)) | If a grotesk is used at all it carries a deliberate, named typographic decision (a custom feature/stylistic set or a paired display face), the way Raycast enables `ss03` for a signature single-story `g`. |
| 6 | Uniform border radius on everything — "same 16px radius and 24px padding" everywhere ([world.hey.com](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)) | A tight, purposeful radius scale (à la Raycast's 4–16px ladder) where each value maps to a specific component type, not one value copy-pasted. |
| 7 | Generic placeholder-brand copy ("scale without limits," "your all-in-one platform," "elevate your workflow") ([world.hey.com](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)) | All copy is specific to Kataki's own characters/stories/mechanics — never abstracted marketing-speak. |
| 8 | Everything shown at once — a table, an input panel, and an export panel all visible simultaneously, no progressive disclosure ([world.hey.com](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)) | Sky and Scene are deliberately two *separate* modes rather than one dashboard trying to show browsing and chatting at once. |
| 9 | Sparkle (✨) icon standing in for "this is AI" on every AI-adjacent control, diluting meaning through overuse ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/ai-sparkle-icon-research-pozos-schmidt)) | Kataki *is* the AI — there's no separate "AI feature" to badge with a sparkle; if any indicator is needed it should be specific (e.g., a character's own mood/state cue), not a generic ✨. |
| 10 | Missing or undesigned empty/error/loading states — the "happy path only" tell ([world.hey.com](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)) | Empty Sky (no characters yet), a character "thinking," and connection errors are each designed as real states with their own small illustration/copy, not left blank. |
| 11 | Decorative hover bounce/transform on everything ([SmoothUI](https://smoothui.dev/blog/ai-design-slop), [no-slop-ui](https://github.com/LeoStehlik/no-slop-ui)) | Motion is reserved for state changes (message arriving, portrait reacting, Sky→Scene transition) — nothing animates just to prove it's interactive. |
| 12 | A "dashboard" reflex — metric-card grids and hero banners inside what should be a working surface ([no-slop-ui](https://github.com/LeoStehlik/no-slop-ui)) | Kataki has no dashboard; Sky is a browse surface, Scene is a reading/chat surface — neither borrows dashboard conventions. |

## 4. Craft references

| Product | What to study | What Kataki takes |
|---|---|---|
| **Linear** | A fully dark-first system built from a 4–5-step near-black surface ladder (`#010102` canvas up through `#18191a`) instead of drop shadows; one lavender accent (`#5e6ad2`) used sparingly; aggressive negative letter-tracking on display type paired with a calm 16px/1.5 body — "single voice from display to body" ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design/); design tokens via [awesome-design-md](https://github.com/voltagent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/linear.app/DESIGN.md)) | The surface-ladder-not-shadow method for depth; one accent color max in Scene's functional chrome; large confident display type against quiet body text. |
| **Raycast** | An entirely dark, no-light-mode product where the *only* saturated color lives inside extension icons/illustrations, never in chrome; a tight radius scale (4px keycaps → 16px hero panels, pills reserved for exactly two uses); Inter with a custom `ss03` stylistic set as a deliberate signature detail, not a default ([awesome-design-md/raycast](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/blob/main/design-md/raycast/DESIGN.md)) | The discipline of "color lives in the content, not the frame" — Kataki's painted character art can be the only saturated color in an otherwise restrained Sky shell. |
| **Dia (Browser Company)** | Deliberately un-radical chrome — familiar tab/bookmark patterns "elevated" with small humanizing touches (page titles instead of raw URLs, contextual hover-only controls) — while spending its entire "novelty budget" on one high-personality surface (the AI Chat panel), which alone gets bold animation and color ([Browser Company Substack](https://browsercompany.substack.com/p/the-strategy-behind-dias-design)) | Exactly Kataki's two-register logic: keep Sky's navigation shell quiet and conventional so users never have to relearn basic wayfinding, and spend all the personality budget inside the Scene, where the story actually lives. |
| **Raycast / Linear (shared pattern)** | Both reject drop-shadow depth entirely in favor of flat surface steps — a specifically "quiet" aesthetic the anti-slop literature explicitly cites as the anti-pattern of "AI-slop" dashboards ([no-slop-ui](https://github.com/LeoStehlik/no-slop-ui) names Linear/Raycast/Stripe/GitHub as its craft model) | Validates surface-stepping as the load-bearing depth technique for Kataki's Sky UI chrome. |
| **Things 3** | Long-standing reference for a to-do app that treats whitespace, a restrained accent palette per list, and subtle motion (checkmark micro-animation, magic plus button) as the entire personality of the product, with no illustration needed to feel warm ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/things-3/id904237743); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Things_(software))) | Proof that warmth and "delight" can come from typography, spacing and one well-placed motion detail rather than decoration — useful discipline for Sky's simpler screens (settings, character list). |
| **Obsidian** | A near-blank canvas core app whose visual identity is almost entirely delegated to a community theme/plugin layer (hundreds of appearance plugins), meaning the "chrome" itself stays deliberately neutral so user content (notes, in Kataki's case: story art and prose) can carry all the visual weight ([Obsidian Publish hub](https://publish.obsidian.md/hub/02+-+Community+Expansions/02.01+Plugins+by+Category/Style+and+Appearance+Plugins)) | Reinforces: Scene's UI frame should recede so the painted world and character portraits do the visual talking, not compete with it. |
| **Figma** | Systematic, token-driven typography (documented variable/token workflows for type scales) as the backbone of a tool used by professional designers all day — legibility and scale discipline over decoration ([Figma best practices](https://www.figma.com/best-practices/typography-systems-in-systems-in-figma/)) | A disciplined, named type scale (not ad hoc sizes) for both Sky and Scene, so headings/body/UI labels stay consistent across two very different visual moods. |
| **Cron / Notion Calendar** | A calendar product whose visual identity is built from restrained color-coding per calendar plus a light, almost editorial typographic treatment of dates/times, rather than icon-heavy chrome ([Cron blog](https://www.cron.com/blog); [Notion Calendar review](https://efficient.app/apps/notion-calendar)) | Supports using color as *information* (whose story, which character) rather than as brand decoration in Sky's browse grid. |

## 5. Art-led interface references

**Disco Elysium** — the writing team explicitly modeled its dialogue/thought-cabinet text flow on how people read social feeds: "people don't actually read the text of the tweet, they go through and simply get the main idea of it," so dialogue "tumbles up and is managed as a column text" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/disco-elysium-working-on-ui-design)). The lesson for Kataki isn't the specific reference (Twitter) but the underlying move: borrowing a *reading rhythm* people already know (a chat/feed cadence) and layering painted, painterly art and UI chrome around it, rather than inventing a novel reading mechanic that fights the eye.

**80 Days (inkle)** — deliberately structured its text as back-and-forth exchange rather than blocks: "you say something, the game says something back, then you say something, and so forth," per creative director Jon Ingold, with a touch-to-continue mechanic designed so revealing text feels like something the player is doing, not something dumped on them ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-80-days-i-building-the-perfect-text-adventure-for-mobile)). Its city artwork was grounded in real research (illustrator "Jaume" sourced actual 1872-era buildings/landmarks per city). For Kataki: this validates a conversational, paced reveal for Scene dialogue over a scrolling wall of text, and argues for character/location art that's *researched and specific* to each story rather than generic fantasy-portrait filler.

**Sunless Sea (Failbetter Games)** — the art direction brief was explicit and disciplined: keep the established "cartoony but spooky" tone, use light itself as both a gameplay mechanic and the atmosphere generator (searchlights, glim-lights, bioluminescent creatures in an otherwise dark Unterzee), and hold to a *restrained*, chosen palette (tealy-green water, icy-blue lamps, brass/iron interface tones) specifically so creatures and interface elements stay legible against a dark backdrop ([Failbetter Games](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/caverns-measureless-to-man-or-sunless-sea-art-direction)). This is close to a direct template for Kataki's Scene: a dark, atmospheric base, a small number of chosen accent hues (not a full rainbow), and functional UI colors (brass/iron in their case) treated as part of the *painted* palette rather than imported from a generic UI kit.

**Streaming-era key art / editorial poster design** — the shift Netflix and streaming forced on poster designers is instructive: art now has to read "equally intriguing at the scale of a billboard or the size of a thumbnail," and the best studios responded with more specific, more typographically confident compositions (deliberately unconventional type placement) rather than the old "floating heads and explosions" genre-formula cliché ([AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/the-art-of-the-movie-poster-in-the-age-of-netflix/)). For Kataki's Sky (a browse grid of characters/stories), this is the direct analogy: each character card is effectively key art and has to work small in a grid *and* full-size when opened — meaning character portraits need real compositional thought (not a centered bust shot with a gradient background), and any card typography should be considered, not a generic label stamped on top.

**Magazine/editorial web design (general pattern, supported by the trend research above)** — the throughline across Linear's "single typographic voice," Things 3's restraint, and the streaming key-art shift is the same editorial instinct: asymmetry, one strong focal element, and confident negative space read as *designed*, where perfectly even grids of same-weight elements read as templated. This is the connective tissue between "craft reference" and "art-led reference" for Kataki — both traditions reward a visible point of view over safe uniformity.

## 6. What this means for Kataki

1. **Two palettes, two temperatures, one type system.** Sky can run brighter/warmer (daylight, social) per the Material-3-Expressive "consumer products go saturated" pattern; Scene pulls toward the Linear/Raycast/Sunless Sea restraint (a small, chosen set of hues, dark-capable). Both share one typographic system so the app still feels like one product.
2. **Reserve glass for Scene, never make it Sky's default chrome.** Liquid Glass's own backlash is the warning label: translucency over painted art (Scene) works; translucency over functional navigation and lists (Sky) is where Apple got burned on legibility.
3. **Depth via a surface ladder, not drop-shadow glow.** Copy Linear/Raycast's method of stepped near-flat surface tones for elevation; a glow-under-every-card look is now a named AI-slop tell, not a craft signal.
4. **One accent color rule for functional chrome; let the art carry color.** Raycast's discipline — saturated hues live only in illustration/content, never in the frame — maps directly onto "let character portraits be the color, not the UI."
5. **Motion must explain a state change, or it doesn't ship.** This is the single most repeated, most confidently stated point across current-trend sources; every Kataki transition (Sky↔Scene, a character's reaction, a message arriving) needs a functional justification, not just polish.
6. **Typography does the "expressive" work, not shape-morphing or gradients.** One confident display face for character names/headers, aggressive negative tracking allowed there, calm body text underneath — this alone can carry most of the "expressive/2026" trend without borrowing Material 3's platform-specific componentry.
7. **A tight, purposeful border-radius scale (roughly 4–16px) with pills reserved for one or two specific roles** (primary action, status), never applied uniformly — uniform radius is a named slop tell.
8. **Dark mode is a first-class Scene mode, built as its own palette, not an inversion.** Treat it the way Linear treats its whole product: dark-first, with warmth (a faint tint, not pure black).
9. **Every screen needs its empty/loading/error state designed on purpose** (an empty Sky, a "character is composing," a connection failure) — the "happy path only" gap is one of the clearest slop tells and also where a painterly app can add real character-driven charm (a sketch instead of a blank state).
10. **Character/story cards are key art, not stat tiles.** Treat the Sky browse grid like a streaming/poster problem — legible at thumbnail size, no floating-head-on-gradient cliché, a real compositional choice for every card — and use asymmetric (bento-style) sizing to signal importance, not a uniform repeating grid.
11. **Borrow reading rhythm before borrowing visual style.** Disco Elysium and 80 Days both succeeded by pacing text like a conversation, not a document; Kataki's Scene should design the *cadence* of message reveal (touch/tap-to-continue, conversational back-and-forth) before worrying about chrome.
12. **No sparkle-icon, no "AI feature" badge.** Kataki's entire premise is the AI character — there is no separate assistant feature to flag with a ✨; any indicator of a character's internal state should be bespoke to that character, not a generic AI glyph.
