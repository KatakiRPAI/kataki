# What makes a human mind and human conversational behaviour "human": taxonomy for simulating believable characters (Phase 1)

Scope note: this is the phase-1 taxonomy. Every cited finding below was seen in a search result during this session (as of September 2026). Classic anchors that I could not source in this pass (Kahneman's System 1/2 book, Vygotsky, Maslow, Lazarus, Big Five facet structure, jealousy, humour, embodiment, identity) are listed under Gaps, not asserted as cited fact. Items marked "Inference" are my own reasoning, not sourced claims.

## 1. Dual-process cognition, inner speech, mind-wandering, incubation: what does "subconscious thinking" look like scientifically?

### Takeaway
"Subconscious thinking" is best modelled as three separable things: fast autonomous Type 1 processing that yields default answers unless Type 2 intervenes, spontaneous thought that wanders under weak constraints (about half of waking time), and offline incubation that helps some problem types after a break. Inner speech is a real, varied, dialogic, condensed process that is tied to anxiety and evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- Evans and Stanovich's preferred dual-process account: rapid autonomous Type 1 processes yield default responses unless intervened on by distinctive higher-order Type 2 reasoning. They also argue the theories should not be treated as one monolithic "System 1/System 2" category. — [Evans & Stanovich 2013, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691612460685); [PhilPapers entry](https://philpapers.org/rec/EVADTO)
- Christoff et al. (2016): mind-wandering is one member of a family of spontaneous-thought phenomena that includes creative thought and dreaming. Its content and transitions are limited by deliberate constraints (cognitive control) and automatic constraints (sensory or affective salience). — [Nature Reviews Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn.2016.113)
- The default mode network is active at rest and is recruited during spontaneous mind-wandering when meta-awareness and deliberate constraint are weak. Mind-wandering also engages executive regions such as dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, so it is not purely "default" activity. — [Christoff et al. 2016 PDF](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Christoff-et-al.-2016.pdf); [Christoff lab page](https://www.christofflab.ca/spontaneous-thought/)
- A recent review links the default mode network to both spontaneous thought and memory consolidation. — [ScienceDirect review, "The default mode network: where spontaneous thought meets memory consolidation"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S235215462500141X)
- Killingsworth and Gilbert (2010), about 250,000 experience samples via iPhone app: minds wandered 46.9% of the time, at least 30% of the time in every activity except sex. People were happiest during sex, exercise and conversation. Time-lag analysis suggested wandering was more cause than consequence of unhappiness. — [Harvard Gazette summary](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2010/11/wandering-mind-not-a-happy-mind/); [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1192439)
- Incubation meta-analysis (Sio & Ormerod 2009): a positive incubation effect exists. Divergent-thinking tasks benefit more than linguistic or visual insight tasks. Longer preparation periods help more. High-demand filler tasks during the break reduce the effect. Low-demand filler tasks beat plain rest for linguistic insight problems. — [Psychological Bulletin 2009 (PsycNet)](https://psycnet.apa.org/fulltext/2008-18777-009.html); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Does-incubation-enhance-problem-solving-A-review.-Sio-Ormerod/76c6ce0abf6f67f2c089df372261203989b43903)
- Inner speech varies along dialogicality (to-and-fro like conversation), condensation (abbreviated versus expanded), evaluative/motivational content, and incorporation of other people's voices. Anxiety, but not depression, was uniquely related to evaluative/motivational inner speech and to other voices in inner speech. — [McCarthy-Jones & Fernyhough, VISQ](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810011001954); [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/MCCTVO-3)
- Recent synthesis: inner speech is a functional language process and cognitive tool with varied subjective qualities, and it can benefit cognition in several domains. — [Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2023](https://www.cell.com/trends/cognitive-sciences/fulltext/S1364-6613(23)00210-3); also [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Inner Speech](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/inner-speech/)

### Inferences
- A character's "peek into thoughts" view is scientifically closest to condensed, dialogic, evaluative inner speech, not to full polished sentences. Anxious characters should have more self-evaluation and imagined other voices.
- Incubation implies a time skip or off-screen interval should improve creative or divergent solutions more than logical ones, and low-demand activity between scenes should help more than idle waiting.
- Wandering at 30-47% of the time means a character who is always fully on-topic reads as inhuman. Wandering should be modulated by boredom, emotional salience and concern, per Christoff's automatic constraints.
- Type 1 default followed by Type 2 override maps onto a two-pass character turn: a quick gut reply, then a possible revision.

### Gaps
- Kahneman's System 1/System 2 formulation and Vygotsky's private speech: I did not fetch a primary source; only Evans and Stanovich's critique-aware version was cited.
- Whether "unconscious thought" advantages (Dijksterhuis) replicate: not searched. The incubation meta-analysis is cited instead, and its effect is moderate and task-dependent.
- Recent (2020-2026) mind-wandering work beyond the DMN-consolidation review: not covered.

## 2. Emotion: appraisal, core affect, mood, anxiety, regulation

### Takeaway
Emotion is best modelled as a pipeline: continuous appraisal produces changes in a two-dimensional core affect (valence and arousal), which is then labelled and regulated. Anxiety is repetitive, largely uncontrollable negative thinking. Regulation choice (reappraisal versus suppression) changes both experience and social outcome.

### Cited Findings
- OCC model (Ortony, Clore, Collins): emotions are differentiated by appraisal patterns over three construals: events, agents' responsibility, and objects. It is described as "computationally friendly" and has been updated to a psychological-construction reading. — [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cognitive-structure-of-emotions/87ED4D16E77B2DFF433F3400DB3C0D34); [Clore & Ortony 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1754073913489751); [NPC-emotion review, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10031)
- Scherer's Component Process Model: appraisal runs through four sequential checks (relevance, implications, coping potential, normative significance) and drives synchronised changes in physiology, motivation, expression and feeling. Appraisal is continuous and re-appraisal can change the emotion. — [Penn State open textbook on CPM](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/component-process-model-cpm/); [Scherer 2009](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02699930902928969)
- Russell's core affect: a single simple feeling of good/bad and energised/enervated. It is the heart, but not the whole, of mood and emotion. An emotion episode starts when an event changes core affect, then appraisal, behaviour and labelling follow. — [Russell 2003, Psychological Review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10949787_Core_Affect_and_the_Psychological_Construction_of_Emotion); [Russell 2009](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02699930902809375)
- Gross process model: reappraisal acts early and reduces experience and expression with no memory cost. Suppression acts late, reduces expression but not experience, impairs memory, raises physiological arousal for the suppressor and their partner, and is associated with worse interpersonal functioning. — [Gross 2002, Psychophysiology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1017/S0048577201393198); [Penn State textbook summary](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/process-model-of-emotion-regulation/)
- Worry and rumination are forms of repetitive negative thinking, a transdiagnostic process. Worry is uncontrollable, negatively charged thought about possible future threats. Rumination focuses on symptoms and their implications. Both are elevated across emotional disorders. — [Nature Reviews Psychology 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-024-00399-6); [Ehring & Watkins-related transdiagnostic study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S000579671000255X); [Worry/rumination/comorbidity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032713004928)

### Inferences
- An OCC-style event/agent/object appraisal, followed by an update of a valence-arousal core-affect vector that decays into a slower mood, matches the sources and is cheap to simulate.
- Emotional contagion, mood versus temperament, and the Lazarus primary/secondary appraisal split are not cited here (see Gaps); the three-timescale idea (episode, mood, temperament) is background knowledge.
- Anxious characters should show inner-speech worry loops (anticipatory, future-oriented) rather than only anxious words in dialogue. Suppression by a character should leak: shorter replies and somewhat worse recall of the moment.

### Gaps
- Emotional contagion, Lazarus, and mood-versus-emotion-versus-temperament distinctions: no source retrieved.
- Anticipatory anxiety versus rumination as distinct timing (future versus past) is only implied by the worry and rumination definitions above.

## 3. Personality: Big Five/HEXACO, interpersonal circumplex, attachment, state-trait variation

### Takeaway
Use a trait profile as the centre of a distribution, not a fixed output. People routinely show almost the whole range of every trait within a couple of weeks, yet their average, spread and shape are stable. Dominance/warmth (interpersonal circumplex) and HEXACO's Honesty-Humility are the most direct handles for bratty, submissive, blunt and manipulative styles.

### Cited Findings
- Whole Trait Theory (Fleeson): a trait is the density distribution of states. Within-person variability is high (the typical person shows nearly all levels of all Big Five traits in daily behaviour), while individual differences in central tendency are almost perfectly stable. The amount of variability, skew and kurtosis are themselves stable individual differences, and variability in extraversion reflects reactivity to situational cues. — [Fleeson 2001, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11414368/); [Fleeson lab summary](https://fleesonlab.wordpress.com/whole-trait-theory/); [Fleeson & Jayawickreme 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/08902070251366709)
- Interpersonal circumplex: organised on two axes, agency/dominance and communion/warmth, formalised by Wiggins in 1979. — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpersonal_circumplex); [Gurtman 2009](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2009.00172.x)
- HEXACO adds Honesty-Humility to the Big Five as a sixth factor (H, E, X, A, C, O) with 24 facets. The Big Five, HEXACO and circumplex can be aligned in a common circular interpersonal space. — [Ashton & Lee 2008](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2008.00134.x); [Mapping the interpersonal domain](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886915003773); [hexaco.org](https://hexaco.org/references)
- Adult attachment is now mostly measured as two continuous dimensions, attachment anxiety and avoidance (ECR, ECR-R), rather than categories. — [Fraley overview](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~rcfraley/attachment.htm); [Shaver 2009](https://adultattachment.faculty.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/66/2015/09/Shaver_2009_Attachment-Theory-and-Attachment-Styles.pdf)
- LLM-specific, preprint: on the "Chameleon" dataset, 74% of psychological variance in user interactions was within-person (state) and 26% between-person (trait). The authors report that language models are "state-blind": they respond to the trait and give similar replies regardless of state. — [arXiv 2601.15395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15395) (single preprint, not peer-reviewed as far as I saw)

### Inferences
- Persona prompts that fix a trait label will produce the "state-blind" flat character the preprint describes. The character needs an explicit current-state layer (mood, need level, recent events) that shifts behaviour around a stable trait mean.
- Brat, submissive, dominant, blunt and sugarcoating map onto dominance (vertical) by warmth (horizontal) position plus Honesty-Humility and Agreeableness facets; each character should also have a variability width, not just a mean.
- Attachment anxiety/avoidance predicts behaviour in jealousy, reassurance-seeking, withdrawal and reply-latency scenarios; it is the most relationship-specific personality layer.

### Gaps
- Big Five facet-level detail (NEO/BFAS) and evidence linking specific facets to bratty/submissive behaviour: not retrieved; the circumplex mapping is inference.
- No peer-reviewed 2020-2026 evidence on stable-versus-state simulation in LLM characters beyond the one preprint.

## 4. Motivation and needs: Maslow, SDT, goals, homeostatic drives

### Takeaway
Self-determination theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness) is the best-supported motivational frame for a character's wants and reactions. Homeostatic needs (hunger, fatigue, sleep, circadian) are not covered by any source retrieved in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- SDT holds that autonomy, competence and relatedness are innate needs. Satisfying them predicts intrinsic motivation and well-being, and thwarting them harms motivation, performance and mental health. The type of motivation matters more than its amount. Overjustification: external rewards for an already-enjoyed activity can reduce later interest. — [APA overview of SDT](https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/self-determination-theory); [Simply Psychology summary](https://www.simplypsychology.org/self-determination-theory.html)

### Inferences
- Autonomy thwarting is a natural trigger for brattiness or reactance; relatedness thwarting for withdrawal or clinginess; competence thwarting for defensiveness. These give the character reasons for behaviour rather than random mood shifts.
- Tiredness and hunger as slowly varying state variables (background knowledge) would feed the state layer in section 3 and the time-skip mechanic; they need their own sourcing.

### Gaps
- Maslow's hierarchy (and its empirical weaknesses), goal-setting theory, drive theory, circadian and sleep-deprivation effects on mood and cognition: no source retrieved.

## 5. Memory: types, forgetting, reconsolidation, false memory, retrieval failure

### Takeaway
Human memory is reconstructive, not a log. Forgetting follows a replicable curve with a possible bump after sleep, retrieving a memory makes it modifiable, and failures (blocking, misattribution, suggestibility, bias) are ordinary. Tip-of-the-tongue states are partial access with accurate partial knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- Murre and Dros (2015) replicated Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve (intervals 20 minutes to 31 days), found results similar to the original, and reported the curve is not completely smooth and probably jumps upward from the 24-hour point. — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0120644); [PMC copy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4492928/)
- Reconsolidation: a consolidated memory becomes labile when retrieved and must be re-stabilised, which allows updating or disruption (Nader, Schafe and LeDoux 2000, in rat fear memory). — [Nature 2000](https://www.nature.com/articles/35021052); [Nader, Schafe, LeDoux review](https://njc.rockefeller.edu/PDF_BN08/Topic%201%20NaderSchafeLeDouxRev.pdf)
- Memory is constructive: recall is shaped by meaning, prior knowledge and reconstruction (Bartlett 1932). Schacter's seven sins are transience, absent-mindedness and blocking (omission), and misattribution, suggestibility, bias and persistence (commission). The DRM paradigm reliably produces false memories from semantically related lists. — [Schacter, seven sins update (Memory 2022)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09658211.2021.1873391); [Constructive memory, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2429996/); [DRM review, Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.668550/full)
- Tip-of-the-tongue: participants can report first letter, syllable count and similar-sounding or similar-meaning words better than chance (Brown and McNeill 1966). The TOT feeling rests on cues such as partial information and cue familiarity, and can change retrieval behaviour. — [Schwartz & Metcalfe review, Memory & Cognition](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-010-0066-8); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21264637/)
- Suppression of emotion impairs memory for the event (Gross). — [Gross 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1017/S0048577201393198)

### Inferences
- Character memory should store gist plus a strength value that decays, gets refreshed on recall, and is rewritten slightly when recalled (a reconsolidation-like update), especially under a new mood. Recalling an event as a lie or white lie can itself overwrite the stored version.
- Believable failures: blocking (partial recall: "it started with an M..."), misattribution (right detail, wrong person), and suggestibility (adopting the user's wording).

### Gaps
- Loftus and Palmer misinformation specifics, emotional-salience effects on encoding, spacing effect, and sleep consolidation as a primary sourced finding (only the DMN-consolidation review title is cited above): not retrieved.
- Episodic/semantic/procedural distinction (Tulving): no source retrieved.

## 6. Social cognition: theory of mind, impression management, politeness, relationship development, trust

### Takeaway
People track others' beliefs both automatically (a limited, fast system) and effortfully (flexible, costly). Everyday talk is shaped by face management (Goffman, Brown and Levinson), and closeness grows through reciprocated, progressively deeper disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- Apperly and Butterfill's two-systems account: an efficient, automatic, early-emerging minimal system tracks belief-like states with signature limits. A flexible, effortful, late-developing system handles full propositional-attitude reasoning. — [Butterfill primer](https://www.butterfill.com/writing/cognitive_architecture_belief_reasoning/); [Butterfill & Apperly, PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/BUTDHH)
- Goffman's dramaturgy: social life is a set of performances; people manage impressions through setting, appearance and manner. Front stage is where we perform, back stage is where we drop the act. Impression management can be conscious or unconscious. — [Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/impression-management.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Presentation_of_Self_in_Everyday_Life)
- Brown and Levinson (1987): positive face is the wish to be liked and approved, negative face the wish for autonomy and not to be imposed on. Face-threatening acts threaten either. The five strategies are bald on-record, positive politeness, negative politeness, off-record (indirect), and not doing the act. — [EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/social-sciences-and-humanities/politeness-theory/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politeness_theory)
- Social penetration theory (Altman and Taylor 1973): relationships deepen via self-disclosure in layers ("onion"); stages run orientation, exploratory affective, affective, stable. Disclosure is reciprocal, and unreciprocated disclosure stalls. Aron et al. (1997) is the experimental closeness-generation procedure. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_penetration_theory); [Rutgers summary](https://sites.comminfo.rutgers.edu/kgreene/wp-content/uploads/sites/28/2018/02/ACGreene-SPT.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Backstage" and "peek" features map neatly onto Goffman: the front-stage utterance differs from the back-stage state. Face-threat weighing (how much does this reply threaten the listener's face, and mine?) can generate sugarcoating, bluntness, hedging and indirectness from one mechanism.
- Bluntness versus sugarcoating is a choice among Brown and Levinson strategies driven by dominance, closeness and the cost of the threat.
- Disclosure depth should be gated by relationship stage and reciprocity, so a stranger character does not overshare on turn two.
- Characters should have limited theory of mind: default assumption that others share their knowledge, with the effortful mindreading step happening only when it matters. This supports believable misunderstandings.

### Gaps
- Trust, reciprocity norms, jealousy, perspective-taking errors (egocentrism): no source retrieved.

## 7. Deception and honesty: everyday lies, white lies, leakage, sugarcoating

### Takeaway
Lying is ordinary and structured: lies to close partners are fewer and more altruistic, lies to acquaintances are more self-serving, and lying makes interactions less pleasant. Prosocial and white lies are judged more ethical and can even raise trust. Liars leak weak cues (less forthcoming, less compelling, more tense), not a single reliable tell.

### Cited Findings
- DePaulo and Kashy (1998): two week-long diary studies (77 undergraduates, 70 community members). Fewer lies per interaction to closer partners, more discomfort when lying to them, more altruistic than self-serving lies to friends and best friends (reverse for acquaintances and strangers), lies to close partners more often discovered, and interactions with a lie were less pleasant and less intimate. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Everyday-lies-in-close-and-casual-relationships.-DePaulo-Kashy/875229407de277adc69186519e70a57e63e3f1bd); [Google Scholar record](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup?hl=en&volume=74&publication_year=1998&pages=63-79&journal=Journal+of+Personality+and+Social+Psychology&author=B.+M.+DePaulo&author=D.+A.+Kashy&title=Everyday+lies+in+close+and+casual+relationships)
- People are more willing to tell prosocial than selfish lies (Erat and Gneezy 2012) and see prosocial lies as more ethical (Levine and Schweitzer 2014). A white lie can signal prosocial preference and raise trust (Levine and Schweitzer 2015), though only altruistic, not Pareto, white lies reliably reflect prosociality. — [Levine & Schweitzer, Prosocial lies (OBHDP)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Prosocial-Lies-OBHDP.pdf); [Does telling white lies signal pro-social preferences?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.03973); [Replication of Erat & Gneezy](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167487024000126)
- Cues to deception (DePaulo et al. 2003, 1,338 estimates of 158 cues): liars are less forthcoming, tell less compelling tales, make a more negative impression, are more tense, and include fewer ordinary imperfections and more unusual content. Many behaviours show no or only weak links to deceit. — [Psychological Bulletin 2003 (Duke)](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/660134); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Cues-to-deception.-DePaulo-Lindsay/234a741a8bcdf25f5924101edc1f67f164f5277b); [2023 survey of experts on cues](https://saulkassin.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Luke-et-al.-2023-Cues-Deception.pdf)
- Suppression of expressed emotion is relevant to concealment: it lowers outward expression without lowering felt emotion (Gross). — [Gross 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1017/S0048577201393198)

### Inferences
- Lying should be a per-utterance decision that depends on relationship closeness, the target's face needs, and the character's Honesty-Humility, not a rare scripted event. Close partners get fewer but more altruistic lies.
- Leakage for a lying character: fewer details, shorter answers, tension markers, and (counterintuitively) a tidier story with fewer natural imperfections. Simulating "nervous tells" as certainties would be wrong; the evidence is weak and probabilistic.
- A lie has memory consequences: the character must remember what they said to keep consistent, and later discovery costs trust and intimacy.

### Gaps
- Exact lies-per-day frequency from DePaulo 1996, cross-cultural variation in lying and politeness, and self-deception literature: no source retrieved in this pass.
- No source seen on LLM ability to sustain lies or leak realistically.

## 8. Errors and self-correction: repair, slips, biases, changing one's mind

### Takeaway
Repair is a systematic conversational organisation with a preference for self-correction, not a defect. Memory and reasoning errors are also lawful (Schacter's sins, Type 1 defaults), which makes them simulatable as patterned, not random.

### Cited Findings
- Schegloff, Jefferson and Sacks (1977): an organisation of repair handles recurrent problems in speaking, hearing and understanding; self-repair (by the speaker of the trouble source) predominates over other-repair, and there is a preference for self-correction. Initiation and completion can each be by self or other. — [Language 1977, Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language/article/abs/preference-for-selfcorrection-in-the-organization-of-repair-in-conversation/5549B861FDE7180B75FA5C382821875E); [ISCA archive](https://www.conversationanalysis.org/schegloff-media-archive/preference-for-self-correction-in-repair-in-conversation-1977/)
- Type 1 defaults can be overridden by Type 2, which gives the "first answer wrong, then corrected" pattern. — [Evans & Stanovich 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691612460685)
- Memory failures of misattribution, suggestibility and bias are normal. — [Schacter update](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09658211.2021.1873391)
- Tip-of-the-tongue states produce visible retrieval struggle with partial knowledge. — [Schwartz & Metcalfe](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-010-0066-8)

### Inferences
- Realistic self-repair takes the forms: mid-utterance restart ("I went on Tuesday, no, Wednesday"), delayed self-correction a turn or two later, and other-initiated repair by the user ("wait, you said..."). Most should be self-initiated.
- Error generation should draw on memory-failure types and the Type 1 gut-then-override pattern, with confidence not perfectly tied to accuracy.
- Changing one's mind should be gated by the character's openness and dominance and by face concerns, since backing down costs face.

### Gaps
- Speech-error taxonomy (Freudian versus Garrett-style slips), cognitive bias catalogue and overconfidence evidence, and belief updating literature: no source retrieved.
- Quantitative repair rates per turn: not retrieved.

## 9. Conversational realism: turn-taking, disfluency, latency, initiative, silence, texting

### Takeaway
Turn-taking is tightly timed (typical gap about 200 ms across languages) and requires prediction of the other's turn end. Filled pauses ("uh", "um") are meaningful signals of planned delays. Text chat has different timing norms that I could not source.

### Cited Findings
- Stivers et al. (2009), 10 languages: all show a unimodal peak of response offsets within 200 ms of question end. Overlap is avoided and silence is minimised, with cultural variation in mean gaps within roughly 250 ms of the cross-language mean. — [PNAS 2009](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0903616106); [PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2705608/)
- Levinson and Torreira (2015): gaps are about 200 ms but language production takes 600 ms or more, so listeners must predict turn end and begin planning while still listening. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full); [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/LEVTIT-8)
- Clark and Fox Tree (2002): "uh" announces a minor expected delay and "um" a major one. Speakers use them to signal word search, deciding what to say, holding the floor or ceding it. — [Cognition 2002 PDF](http://www.columbia.edu/~rmk7/HC/HC_Readings/Clark_Fox.pdf); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Using-uh-and-um-in-spontaneous-speaking-Clark-Tree/2031989919682a512f255e0176969943548a21b0)
- Turn-taking varies idiosyncratically and by dyad and demographics (recent preprint on "dyadosyncrasy"). — [arXiv 2505.24736](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24736)

### Inferences
- In a text app the 200 ms speech gap does not transfer directly. What transfers is that latency is meaningful: a long delay before a face-threatening reply, or a fast reply that shows eagerness, communicates state. Variable typing indicators and interruptions of a half-written message can carry this.
- Disfluencies ("um", trailing off, "...") should be used as signals of delay, search, hesitation or a face-threatening move, not sprinkled as decoration.
- Initiative (starting topics, texting first, drifting topics) should be driven by needs, boredom and the wandering model, not only by the user's last message.

### Gaps
- Texting-specific behaviour (latency norms, typos, message splitting, read receipts, emoji), backchannels, silence tolerance across cultures, and topic drift statistics: no reliable source retrieved.

## 10. Anything else that makes humans feel human (values, identity, inconsistency, off-screen life) and the prioritized table

### Takeaway
Beyond the listed items, values (moral foundations), within-person inconsistency, and continuity of a life off-screen are the best-supported extras; humour, embodiment, habits, curiosity, boredom and narrative identity are plausible but unsourced in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Moral Foundations Theory: five (originally) moral intuitions, namely harm/care, fairness/reciprocity, ingroup/loyalty, authority/respect and purity/sanctity, offered as an explanation for moral disagreement. It is contested, including replication concerns in some samples. — [Graham et al., Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Moral-Foundations-Theory:-The-Pragmatic-Validity-of-Graham-Haidt/a81893fbdbaf2cead51f6886408c9c792e00d362); [Springer entry](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-28099-8_1253-1); [Critique and reception summary](https://yukaichou.com/behavioral-analysis/moral-foundations-theory-haidt-care-fairness-loyalty/)
- Within-person inconsistency is the norm and is stable in its own shape (Whole Trait Theory), and LLM personas tend to ignore state. — [Fleeson lab](https://fleesonlab.wordpress.com/whole-trait-theory/); [arXiv 2601.15395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15395)
- Mind-wandering during about half of waking life means a person has an ongoing mental life independent of the current topic. — [Killingsworth & Gilbert, Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2010/11/wandering-mind-not-a-happy-mind/)

### Inferences
- Values give a stable basis for moral conflict, moral disgust and loyalty in scenes, and for what a character will refuse to lie about. Values are stable while mood and needs vary.
- "Having a life off-screen" can be implemented through time skips plus a background thought stream (worries, plans, unfinished business) that surfaces in later scenes. This is the same mechanism as incubation and mind-wandering.

### Gaps
- Humour, curiosity, boredom, embodiment/habit, narrative identity and self-concept (McAdams), growth over time (personality development): no source retrieved. Treat as candidates for phase 2.

### Prioritized table

Ranking is my judgment of believability impact given the sources above; simulatability notes are inference, not sourced findings.

| Trait / component | Conversational behaviour | Believability impact | Simulatability note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Situational state layer over stable traits (Fleeson; state-blind LLMs) | Same character replies differently when tired, hurt, distracted, happy | High | Explicit state vector injected each turn; addresses the documented state-blindness. |
| Emotion via appraisal to core affect to mood (OCC, Scherer, Russell) | Mood carries between turns, reactions proportionate and explainable | High | Small appraisal step plus valence/arousal with decay; cheap. |
| Face management and politeness (Goffman, Brown and Levinson) | Sugarcoating, hedging, bluntness, indirect refusals, saving face | High | One "face-threat" weighing step per reply picks among the five strategies. |
| Lying and honesty by relationship (DePaulo; prosocial lies) | White lies, omissions, altruistic lies to friends, self-serving lies to strangers | High | Per-reply decision using closeness, Honesty-Humility, stakes; needs lie-consistency memory. |
| Dominance and warmth position (interpersonal circumplex) | Bratty, submissive, dominant, or warm behaviour | High | Two numeric axes plus style exemplars; LLMs follow these well. |
| Memory: decay, reconsolidation, distortion (Ebbinghaus, Schacter) | Forgets details, misattributes, recalls differently later | High | Strength-weighted memory store with lossy recall; requires memory infrastructure. |
| Self-repair and error (Schegloff et al.) | Restarts, "no wait", delayed corrections, being corrected by the user | Medium-High | Post-hoc or in-stream text patterns; risk of sounding scripted if overused. |
| Inner speech and anxiety loops (Fernyhough; worry/rumination) | Peek view shows condensed, evaluative, dialogic thought; anxious spirals | Medium-High | A separate thought-generation pass; sits naturally in the "peek" feature. |
| Wandering, incubation, off-screen life (Christoff; Sio and Ormerod) | Comes back after time skip with a new idea or preoccupation; drifts off topic | Medium-High | Background thought queue run during time skips; cost is one extra call per skip. |
| Emotion regulation (Gross) | Reappraises or suppresses; suppression leaks and harms rapport | Medium | Regulation strategy as a trait-driven tag on the reply. |
| Needs and motives (SDT) | Reactance when autonomy thwarted; seeks connection; competence defensiveness | Medium | Three need meters shifted by scene events. |
| Relationship development (social penetration; attachment) | Gradual disclosure, reciprocity, reassurance-seeking or avoidance | Medium | Relationship stage variable gating disclosure; attachment as two numbers. |
| Limited theory of mind | Misreads the user, false assumptions about what others know | Medium | Prompt the character with only what it could plausibly know; enforce per-character knowledge boundaries. |
| Disfluency and latency (Clark and Fox Tree; Stivers) | Hesitation markers, delays that mean something, "um" before hard answers | Medium | Text markers and typing-delay UI; do not overuse. |
| Values (moral foundations) | Refuses, condemns, defends on principle | Medium | A short values list in the character card. |
