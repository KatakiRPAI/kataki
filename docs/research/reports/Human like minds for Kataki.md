# Build Minds in Code, Let Models Speak

Kataki can move past context-aware roleplay by giving each character a small, persistent, inspectable mind that deterministic code owns and a language model only voices. That mind has five parts: moods that decay on the story clock, a relationship ledger that holds grudges, secrets with cover stories, memories that fade and drift, and a life between scenes. The evidence points one way: what reads as human is state carried across turns plus imperfections with a cause, not a smarter model or longer reasoning. Generative Agents lost believability every time memory, reflection or planning was removed, while chain-of-thought and "thinking" modes lowered roleplay and villain scores ([Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442); [arXiv 2502.16940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16940); [Moral RolePlay](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04962v1)). No hosted competitor shows a character's private thoughts, motives or lies. Only one small open-source project, Front Porch AI, simulates needs and moods ([Front Porch AI](https://github.com/Lufou/front-porch-AI)), so an inspectable inner life is still unclaimed ground. The unified design for Kataki's existing engine costs one reply call plus at most one small closed-enum side call per turn, and one background call per tracked character per time skip. That fits the default 8 GB laptop running Qwen3.5-9B. On a cheap cloud stack it costs about $0.58 a month for a light user and $11.63 for a 1,000-message-a-day whale, so a free local app plus a $7.99 hosted plan keeps 63-78% margin. The main caveats: nearly every latency, threshold and margin figure is an estimate, much of the evidence was read at abstract level on GPT-class models, and no public benchmark tests these behaviours on 7-14B local models. Kataki's own probe set therefore decides what ships.

## Human-ness is state that persists, not smarter replies

The psychology literature sorts the owner's wish list into mechanisms that can be simulated. Minds wander **46.9% of waking time** ([Killingsworth and Gilbert](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2010/11/wandering-mind-not-a-happy-mind/)), so a character who is always on-topic reads as inhuman. Fast Type 1 defaults that a slower Type 2 process sometimes overrides ([Evans and Stanovich](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691612460685)) give the "gut reply, then correction" pattern. Personality is a distribution of states around a stable mean, and within-person variation is large ([Fleeson](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11414368/)). One preprint found **74% of psychological variance in interactions is within-person state**, and called LLMs "state-blind": they answer the trait and ignore the state ([arXiv 2601.15395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15395)). Sugarcoating, hedging, bluntness and white lies all fall out of one face-threat trade-off ([politeness theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politeness_theory)). Everyday lying is ordinary, about one to two lies a day ([DePaulo et al. 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8656340/)). It is also structured by closeness: friends get fewer, kinder lies and strangers get self-serving ones ([DePaulo and Kashy](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Everyday-lies-in-close-and-casual-relationships.-DePaulo-Kashy/875229407de277adc69186519e70a57e63e3f1bd)). Memory is reconstructive and fails in lawful ways ([Schacter](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09658211.2021.1873391)), and conversation repair prefers self-correction ([Schegloff, Jefferson and Sacks](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language/article/abs/preference-for-selfcorrection-in-the-organization-of-repair-in-conversation/5549B861FDE7180B75FA5C382821875E)). The common thread: each "human" behaviour is a lawful product of hidden state, so a system that stores and updates that state can produce it on purpose.

The obstacle is the model's default persona, not missing intelligence. Across 17 models, fidelity falls steadily as a character's morality drops, from **3.21 for paragons to 2.61 for villains**. The hardest traits are deceitful, hypocritical and selfish, and models swap subtle malice for "superficial aggression" ([Moral RolePlay](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04962v1)). In 9 of 13 small open models (0.6-20B), more agreeable personas meant more sycophancy, with **r up to 0.87** ([Too Nice to Tell the Truth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10733)). Resistance collapses over long, emotionally pressured chats even when the model's reasoning still holds the right answer ([SPINE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09090)). Personas drift within about eight turns ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10962)), and emotional-vulnerability contexts pull activations back toward an "Assistant" direction that exists even in base models ([Assistant Axis](https://www.anthropic.com/research/assistant-axis)). The encouraging counterpoint is that style constraints, not raw capability, made GPT-4.5 pass as human: **73% judged human with an introverted, slang-using persona versus 36% without it** ([Jones and Bergen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23674)). Interrogators cited typos and evasiveness, not intelligence ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23674v1)).

These findings shape the rest of the report. Human-ness has to be enforced from outside the model, because the model's training pulls toward warm, agreeable, complete answers. Numbers should live in the app and reach the prompt as behaviour sentences, because LLMs do not separate Big Five factors cleanly: four of five factors collapse into one at r ≥ .90 ([arXiv 2607.02325](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02325)). Negative traits are the weak spot: GPT-3.5 portrayed positive traits 90.7% of the time but negative ones only 61.6% ([PsyPlay](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03821v1)). And model choice matters as much as any prompt layer, because general chat skill does not predict villain skill ([Moral RolePlay](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04962v1)).

## The market sells memory lists; nobody sells an inner life

Spending is growing and concentrated. Romantic companion apps took **$162.8M in H1 2026** ([Appfigures via Gate](https://www.gate.com/news/detail/ai-romantic-companion-apps-generated-4273m-in-revenue-over-two-years-22609020)), and companion in-app revenue rose about 30% quarter on quarter in Q1 2026 ([Sensor Tower via Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/46285/ai-companion-app-revenue-up-30-percent-zeta-tops-growth-ranking)). The winners are free-to-play volume plays; Chai, for example, claims about $80M ARR ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/chai-ai-backed-by-coreweave-and-amd-hits-80m-arr-with-talks-of-2-4b-valuation-302759626.html)). Paid companions cluster at $10-25 a month; Nomi, for example, charges $15.99 ([Fostera](https://fostera.ai/blog/replika-vs-nomi-vs-kindroid)). These prices come from affiliate-style aggregators and several conflict, so treat them as approximate.

Table stakes are clear: long-term memory with an editable view, a lorebook (Character.AI added one in July 2026 ([aicompanionguides](https://aicompanionguides.com/blog/character-ai-subscription-2026/))), group chat, voice calls, consistent-face images, and proactive messages, which Nomi and Kindroid paywall ([WeavAI](https://weavai.app/blog/en/2026/08/13/proactive-ai-companions-nomi-replika-kindroid-compared/)). The top complaint is memory "context rot" ([Storychat](https://blog.storychat.app/ai-roleplay-persistent-memory-solutions-2026/)), including silent failures such as SillyTavern's summary box staying empty with no error ([Arcanum RPGs](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/sillytavern-memory/)). Sycophancy is a real complaint, but vulnerable users actively seek affirmation ([arXiv 2601.10467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10467v2)). Candour should therefore be a user-controlled dial, not a blanket fix.

What nobody ships is the gap Kataki can own. Every hosted product shows a list of remembered facts, and none shows private thought against spoken words, a hidden motive, a lie and why it was told, or deliberate forgetting with a reason. Front Porch AI is the closest overlap, and it is a direct local-first competitor: AGPL, "a home for Backyard AI refugees". Its Realism Engine has mood inertia, Sims-style needs, fixations, "growth rings" and AFK evolution, but its quality is unmeasured ([Front Porch AI](https://github.com/Lufou/front-porch-AI)). Backyard AI's desktop app is deprecated, which leaves a local-first gap ([Arcanum RPGs](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/sillytavern-alternatives/)). And adults balked at uploading government ID when Character.AI age-gated ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/29/character-ai-is-killing-the-chatbot-experience-for-minors/)), which gives a local-first privacy story real pull. The ranked opportunities are: an inspectable inner life (Peek and Backstage); forgetting that is believable and diagnosable; consistent lies and secrets held against a private truth; per-character model choice inside one group scene; off-screen time with consequences; and sycophancy as a dial. The "nobody does it" judgements come from the absence of evidence in reviews and marketing, not from inspecting closed products.

Regulation is now part of the product. California SB 243 has been in force since 1 January 2026. It requires a clear "not human" notice when a reasonable person could be misled, crisis protocols, and minor protections, with a **$1,000-per-violation private right of action** ([SB 243 text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB243)). New York, Oregon, Washington and others follow ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends)), and the FTC has opened a 6(b) inquiry into how companion personalities are designed ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)). Characters who lie are fine inside an evident fiction; an app that lets a character deny being an AI when sincerely asked is not.

## Nine aspects, one pattern: code decides, the model voices

The nine deep dives reach the same design independently. Put every decision that carries stakes into deterministic code: whether to lie, whether to yield, whether a memory is sharp, who heard what. Put the resulting state into a short verbal block near the end of the prompt, and let the model do what it is good at: rendering a given stance in a character's voice.

### A 25-word thought before speaking beats a reasoning trace

Thinking before speaking has the best human-rated support. Inner Thoughts (CHI 2025) keeps a covert thought stream, scores each thought's motivation to be voiced, and speaks only above a threshold. Raters preferred it **82% of the time** on all seven metrics, but the baseline was weak, there was no ablation, and the chatbot study had only 12 participants ([Inner Thoughts](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383)). Its thoughts are under 15 words and tagged with what triggered them. MIRROR-style thoughts (recall, then think about how others will react, then reflect) raised decision accuracy from 55.17% to 64.39%, but experts found them "less emotionally complex" than real monologues ([MIRROR](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08193)). The negative evidence matters as much: a 24-model study found chain-of-thought can reduce roleplay performance ([arXiv 2502.16940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16940)), and raw reasoning traces cause "attention diversion" and "style drift" ([Role-Aware Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01748)). For Kataki this means a short first-person thought (about 25 words, starting from a feeling rather than analysis) emitted as a hidden header in the same generation. The existing splitter already routes `<think>` spans to Peek and keeps them out of history ([M0/M1 spec](file:///D:/Kataki/docs/specs/2026-09-18-m0-m1-design.md)). Because the thought is generated before the reply and conditions it, Peek is faithful by construction.

"Subconscious" background thinking is where the evidence is thinnest. Offline "sleep-time" compute cut test-time compute about **5x at equal accuracy**, but only when future queries are predictable from context ([Sleep-time Compute](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13171v1)). Roleplay user lines are not predictable, so Kataki should precompute state (worries, plans, unfinished business, incubated ideas) rather than replies. Talker-Reasoner supplies the scheduling trick: the fast talker answers from the latest stored beliefs while a slow reasoner updates them in the background, accepting a delayed view ([Talker-Reasoner](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08328v1)). No study measures whether resurfacing background thoughts raise perceived human-likeness. That feature is a design bet grounded in the psychology of incubation and mind-wandering.

### Moods that decay on the story clock, anxieties that loop

The best-evidenced emotion pattern is "appraise first, then speak" feeding a persistent numeric state. Chain-of-Emotion used two calls per turn and reached **83% on an emotion-understanding test, versus 74% with memory alone and 57% without memory**. In a 30-person study its reactions were rated more natural (p = .03) ([Chain-of-Emotion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11086867/)). A 7-8-call component-process pipeline beat a zero-shot call by only **+0.044 on a 5-point scale** ([CPM-MultiAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07824v1)), which rules multi-agent appraisal out for 8 GB hardware. Sentipolis showed that half-life decay and injecting a label plus a short description (not raw numbers) both measurably helped continuity and believability ([Sentipolis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18027)). ALMA supplies a ready three-timescale recipe (emotion, mood, personality) in pleasure-arousal-dominance space. Its "anxious" octant (−P +A −D) differs from "hostile" (−P +A +D) only in dominance, which is exactly the distinction between anxious and angry sulking ([ALMA](https://alma.dfki.de/papers/aamas05.pdf)). One caution shapes the design: LLMs appraise relevance well but control and certainty poorly, and those are the two dimensions anxiety depends on ([CAREBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17176)). Kataki should therefore derive "can she cope?" and "is the outcome uncertain?" from rules (an unanswered message is uncertain by construction), and let the model supply only the emotion label and intensity.

Anxiety deserves its own small module built on the worry construct: future-oriented, driven by uncertainty, and maintained by avoidance because most worries never happen ([Worry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worry)). A worry is created only when an event is relevant, negative, uncertain and low in control. Reassurance lowers it with a built-in rebound. Rumination is its past-oriented sibling, and attachment anxiety sits on the relationship edge rather than acting as global neuroticism. A trait gate keeps most characters calm. Regulation follows Gross: suppression lowers expression but not feeling and impairs memory, while reappraisal actually lowers the feeling ([Gross 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1017/S0048577201393198)). That gives a real felt-versus-shown gap for Peek, with leaks ("short answers, doesn't meet his eyes") as the tell. Interpretability work shows models already represent unexpressed emotion separately from what they express ([Emotion Concepts in an LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07729v1)). Decay must run on story time in closed form, so "she sulked overnight" costs nothing at a time skip.

### Traits as distributions, and permission to disagree

Personality should be stored as a mean plus a spread per axis (dominance, warmth, candour, honesty, yielding, volatility), resampled per scene, with two attachment numbers. Bratty, submissive, dominant and blunt then become positions on dominance and warmth plus a behaviour kit, not adjectives. Descriptions drive most of trait fidelity: InCharacter found character-specific fine-tuning gave "limited improvement" over description alone ([InCharacter](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17976)). Distinctive traits help more than generic ones ([arXiv 2603.03915](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03915)). The anti-sycophancy toolkit that is actually evidenced is short. It starts with model choice and card wording that drops warm, agreeable words unless intended. RP-Bench, for example, scores holding hard lines against over-refusal and finds single-message and multi-turn rankings invert ([RP-Bench](https://github.com/LeviTheWeasel/rp-benchmark)). Next come a depth-injected persona reminder near the end of the prompt and a yield rule, "change your position only when given a new reason that matters to you". The rule is untested, but it targets the SPINE finding that models know better and cave anyway. The rest is design Kataki must build and probe: a concession budget, a position and grievance ledger, a regex check for agreement openers with one resample, and a user-facing pushback dial (soft, realistic, stubborn). Activation steering is off the table: prompting beat every representation-steering method in AxBench ([AxBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17148)), and llama-server control vectors are startup flags with no per-request control ([llama.cpp issue 10685](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/10685)).

Consent sits outside the character. A 14-day study found supportive roles lifted mood, while antagonist roles gave volatile outcomes and romantic roles turned negative for at-risk users ([Beyond Her](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28968v1)). High-intensity stances therefore warrant a stop word the app intercepts before the model sees it, hard lines enforced by code, and acknowledgement gates.

### Lies need a private truth, a motive and a ledger

Models lie well when a game hands them a hidden role, a goal and a private ground truth. CICERO's planner-plus-dialogue split more than doubled the average human score in Diplomacy ([CICERO](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2022/cicero)). ReCon's "draft, predict how the listener reads it, rewrite" raised Avalon success from **15% to 83.3% with GPT-4** ([ReCon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01320)). Without that structure, aligned models play deceitful characters worst. Small models also collapse on nested beliefs: Hi-ToM accuracy falls toward zero by fourth-order belief ([Hi-ToM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16755)), and even the best model scored only 0.62 on information management in Sotopia-ToM ([Sotopia-ToM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02307)). So Kataki's honesty engine decides in code. A speech-act sampler picks truth, soften, hedge, hint, omit, evade, deflect, white lie, self-serving lie or confess from stakes, closeness, honesty, kindness and face threat, with lie rates kept low to match the one-to-two-a-day base rate. The model gets the finished decision and a canonical cover story. Information Manipulation Theory supplies the move vocabulary, since most everyday deception blends omission, vagueness and deflection rather than outright falsehood ([IMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_manipulation_theory)). Levine and Schweitzer supply the consequences: a discovered kind lie costs integrity-trust but can protect warmth, while a selfish lie costs both ([Levine and Schweitzer](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2266091)). Trivers' self-deception gives the leak-proof variant: a character who sincerely believes the cover story shows no tells ([Trivers](https://www.edge.org/conversation/robert_trivers-deceit-and-self-deception)).

Detection stays in code too, because models produce deception more easily than they detect it ([Among Us](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04072)). Contradictions, witnesses and repeated probing raise suspicion until thresholds fire "doubt" or "caught". In groups, the strongest leak defence is architectural: never put a secret in the prompt of a character who does not hold it. Splitting agents cut private-information leakage by 18-19% in one benchmark ([1-2-3 Check](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07667v2)), and Kataki already generates one speaker at a time from per-knower memory. The legal boundary is simple. Inside the fiction characters may deceive each other and the user's character; the app never lies about what it is. A sincere out-of-character question gets a system-styled truthful answer. That fits SB 243's "reasonable person misled" trigger and the EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure duty ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)). Whether it is enough is for counsel.

### Forgetting on purpose, without ever losing data

Kataki already ships most of a human memory engine, which no competitor does: per-knower knowledge with witnessed, told and overheard sources, ACT-R power-law decay, gist and detail tiers, effortful recall, contradiction challenge, and zero LLM calls per turn for retrieval ([M0/M1 spec](file:///D:/Kataki/docs/specs/2026-09-18-m0-m1-design.md)). External systems add little. Mem0 answers from about 1,764 tokens where full context needs about 26,000 ([Mem0](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)), but vendor leaderboards conflict. Every method fails multi-hop conflict resolution (at most 28%) ([MemoryAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05257)). A plain file-and-grep agent matched specialised systems in Letta's own test ([Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory)). And no benchmark scores forgetting at all.

What is missing are the human effects, each implementable without mutating truth. Recollection variants stored per knower, append-only, cover misremembering. Fuzzy-trace theory shows verbatim decays faster than gist and gist-consistent false memories grow ([Fuzzy-trace theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzzy-trace_theory)), so drift should touch only peripheral details (place, time, who else was there) and never the core or the character's confidence. Retellings that deviate become the character's new version, a cheap analogue of reconsolidation and the misinformation effect ([Misinformation effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misinformation_effect)). Mood-congruent retrieval, which already helped role-play agents ([Emotional RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23041)), needs one valence number per memory. Tip-of-the-tongue partial cues (first letter, place, emotion word) can be built by code from stored data. The trust-critical addition is a reason code on every non-recall that separates "she forgot" (never witnessed, faded, not cued) from "the app lost it" (budget cut, extraction pending). Consolidation into self, relationship and habit lines should follow the Auto-Dreamer pattern ([Auto-Dreamer](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20616v1)): sources stay read-only, output must cite source handles, and it is never a recursive summary of a summary, the shape SillyTavern warns compounds loss ([ST Summarize](https://docs.sillytavern.app/extensions/summarize/)).

### Plant the mistake, then let her fix it

The best-evidenced humanising error is small. Across **N = 3,399**, agents who made and then corrected a typo seemed more human than agents with no typo, while uncorrected typos did nothing. The effect shrank but held when users knew it was a bot (d = 0.60 versus 1.34 when identity was ambiguous) ([Imperfectly Human](https://www.escholarship.org/content/qt7863m2pf/qt7863m2pf_noSplash_77769d18410cf32e93ad9cacc46fc0dc.pdf)). LLMs cannot reliably catch their own errors without external feedback ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798); [Kamoi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297)). So the engine must plant slips where it knows the truth: only in hazy memories, never in canon, at a low rate. It then schedules the repair: a mid-message restart, a follow-up burst, or "wait, it was Thursday" a few turns later. It must also hold the line when the user wrongly "corrects" a true memory, which is the sycophancy guard.

Conversational realism lives in a delivery layer. Delays scaled to message complexity raised perceived humanness and satisfaction ([Gnewuch et al.](https://aisel.aisnet.org/ecis2018_rp/113/)). The Turing-test system used an explicit typing-and-reading delay formula ([Jones and Bergen](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23674v1)). Latency above about 4 s hurt experience in VR agents ([CUI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22352)). Hesitation without a visible edit looked "dumb" in a small study ([Beyond Words](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08912)). For initiative, Inner Thoughts' "active contributor" setting beat both chatty and passive ones, and the chatty variant was flagged as AI 69% of the time ([Inner Thoughts](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383)). Slop is measurable: some patterns are over 1,000 times more common than in human text. A backtracking ban-list costs 69-96% of throughput ([Antislop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)), so the local answer is Min-P ([min-p](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01082)) plus DRY ([DRY](https://github.com/oobabooga/text-generation-webui/pull/5677)) and a short banned list.

### Who-knows-what belongs in a table, grudges in a ledger

Theory-of-mind benchmarks agree that the hard part is deciding who knows what. Models fail when characters enter and leave mid-conversation ([FANToM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15421)). OmniToM names an actor-specific "belief-tracking bottleneck" ([OmniToM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26322)). Filtering context to what a character knows raised Llama-2-7B from **47.5% to 70.5%** on BigToM, while chain-of-thought lowered small-model accuracy in several cells ([SimToM](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.10227)). RL-trained ToM gains did not generalise ([arXiv 2507.15788](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15788)). Kataki does SimToM's first stage in code at zero cost, following Concordia's game-master pattern ([Concordia](https://github.com/google-deepmind/concordia)).

Relationships borrow from games, which have tested these mechanics on players: Sims-style decaying tracks with threshold labels ([Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Relationship)), Crusader Kings opinion modifiers with timers ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Modifiers)), and Persona's lesson that harsh neglect reversals frustrate players ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/same-but-different---comparing-the-social-link-system-in-persona-3-4-5)). The resulting design is an opinion ledger of sourced, decaying modifiers driven by a closed vocabulary of about 24 events (promise broken, sincere versus hollow apology, disclosure reciprocated or not), with per-scene caps, stages that follow social penetration theory, and a realism dial. A grudge is a "sticky" entry that barely decays until a sincere repair flips it to slow decay. Attachment style drives absence reactions, computed lazily at reopen as the gap against the user's usual rhythm: anxious characters seek reassurance, avoidant ones cool ([Attachment in adults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attachment_in_adults)). A per-character "what they believe about you" view with provenance, plus a delete-able "what Mara knows about you" page, answers the privacy risk of companions that infer ever more about users ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/11/24/1128051/the-state-of-ai-chatbot-companions-and-the-future-of-our-privacy/)). Jealousy (attention share against a rival) is design only; no source was found.

### A life between scenes, computed only when you return

Needs work when they become behaviour. In Humanoid Agents, zeroing health changed activity by 156% but zeroing social need changed it by only 12% ([Humanoid Agents results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05418)). Relatedness must therefore become explicit behaviours (seeking contact, asking about the user), not a number the model ignores. Self-determination theory's autonomy, competence and relatedness ([APA](https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/self-determination-theory)) give principled triggers: thwarted autonomy produces reactance and brattiness, thwarted relatedness produces clinginess or withdrawal. Energy comes from a circadian curve with a per-character chronotype. Goals form a three-level stack with at most two live agenda items per scene. Each item is voiced only at an opening and dropped after two deflections, which counters the over-cooperativeness documented in Generative Agents ([Generative Agents](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)).

Off-screen life should be a hybrid, evaluated lazily at a time skip or return rather than by a daemon. Code rolls seeded events with RimWorld-style pacing ([RimWorld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RimWorld)) and writes them into the same memory, goal and mood stores the scenes already read. One small call then only verbalises what happened: a diary line, one or two things worth telling, a preoccupation. Proactive messaging carries the sharpest ethical evidence. **37% of 1,200 real companion-app farewells used manipulative tactics** such as guilt and fear of missing out. Those tactics lifted post-goodbye engagement up to 14x but raised perceived manipulation and churn intent ([HBS working paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)). In a 981-person RCT, heavier voluntary use predicted worse psychosocial outcomes ([arXiv 2503.17473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473)). Kataki's default should therefore be an in-app "while you were away" card. Out-of-app pings should be opt-in, capped at one per character per day, and back off after silence; each must carry a reason and never plead. Growth should follow the evidence-gated "rings" pattern: a change is proposed only when three or more memories across two or more scenes support it, trait moves are capped, and the user can reject it. Human personality changes slowly ([Personality development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_development)), and drift must not masquerade as growth.

### Voice and face follow the same cue, CPU-first

Every top-10 voice on the speech arena is closed; the best open rows sit around Elo 1,050-1,130 ([OfflineTTS](https://www.offlinetts.com/blog/tts-arena-leaderboard-2026/)). For an 8 GB, commercially clean stack the shortlist is three models. Kokoro-82M (Apache 2.0) runs on CPU but has no non-verbal sounds and runs slower than real time on weak ARM cores ([TTS CPU benchmark](https://github.com/obole-ia/tts-cpu-benchmark)). Chatterbox Turbo (350M, MIT) has native [laugh] and [sigh] tags, cloning and a watermark ([model card](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox-turbo)). Orpheus 3B (Apache 2.0) has emotive tags at about 4 GB at Q8 ([Orpheus GGUF](https://huggingface.co/lex-au/Orpheus-3b-FT-Q8_0.gguf)). Voxtral, XTTS-v2, F5 and Fish S2 Pro are non-commercial or research-licensed, and Voxtral needs 16 GB ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-tts/)). The recommended pipeline is cascaded rather than full-duplex, because full-duplex PersonaPlex replaces Kataki's LLM, speaks English only and forgets after about 4 minutes ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/nvidia-personaplex-local-speech-model-8gb-vram/)). One per-line cue (mood, intensity, tags, pause) drives the sprite, the TTS tags and the bubble style, so face and voice agree. Disfluency insertion raised perceived spontaneity at a small cost in intelligibility ([arXiv 2412.12710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12710)), so use it sparingly and only in voice mode.

## One state model, two calls, one background job

Note 22 merges all nine designs onto the existing engine. It reuses what is there: memories and knowledge for beliefs and claims, edges for relationship labels, flags for world state, and `messages.gen` for the per-turn trace. It adds one profile JSON inside the library character, seven small append-only tables (`mind_states`, `opinions`, `seeds`, `secrets`, `goals`, `recollections`, `reflections`) and three memory columns ([schema baseline](file:///D:/Kataki/docs/specs/2026-09-18-m0-m1-design.md); [db.py](file:///D:/Kataki/engine/src/kataki/db.py)). Every new row is anchored to a message or an extraction run, so a swipe or branch swaps the whole mind state the way memory already does. Four rules keep it small: reuse before adding; keep fast-changing numbers in one JSON snapshot per character; derive anything that is a pure function of stored rows (relationship stage, suspicion, alliances); and anchor every row. Closed enums are shared by every call, because small models do best on closed sets: 16 feelings, 24 relationship events (5 detected by code), 11 speech-act moves, 9 seed kinds and 9 gate codes. One design catch is resolved explicitly: needs are not stored as flags, because the engine renders every non-private flag into everyone's `[State]` block, which would leak one character's inner state to the others ([context.py](file:///D:/Kataki/engine/src/kataki/context.py)).

The per-turn pipeline has thirteen steps, and eleven are pure code. The steps are: intercept the stop word and out-of-character questions; perceive who heard the line; tick every decay forward to now; sense the user's line with the already-loaded model2vec embedder against feeling centroids; choose the speaker by a seeded score; run the gate; recall; decide (speech-act move, politeness, yield rule, felt versus shown, slip plan, agenda, length); render the mind block; generate the reply; check for leaks, assistant-isms and thought echo; run the side call; and apply the results. The reply call gains an optional two-line `<think>` header. The side call is one closed-enum JSON on the `utility` model returning the felt label, relationship events, a guess at the user's mood, the sprite face, the claim's move and the agenda outcome. It normally runs after the reply is on screen (lagged and preemptible) and replaces the face call that already runs for characters with sprite packs ([turns.py](file:///D:/Kataki/engine/src/kataki/turns.py)), so it adds zero net calls for them. It moves before the reply only when the code gate fires: a secret is topical, a direct probe, a critical decision word, an affect jump, a stage edge, a first meeting, a stuck conversation, a failed check, or Peek open. That is budgeted to about 15% of turns. The delay costs little because the reply model sees the user's line verbatim; persistent state adds continuity, which tolerates a one-turn lag everywhere except the gate's triggers.

Everything heavier lives in one **Between job** per tracked character per time skip. Phase B0 is a zero-call code tick: decay, worry habituation, absence reactions by attachment style, gossip transfer through knowledge rows, and seeded offstage events. Phase B1 is one call per tracked character (about 1.2-1.5k tokens in, 150-250 out, est. 3-6 s locally). It writes a first-person diary line, up to two things worth telling, one to three thought-seeds and a preoccupation, built only from that character's own memories, because omniscient scene summaries already leaked a secret in Kataki's evals ([M0/M1 spec](file:///D:/Kataki/docs/specs/2026-09-18-m0-m1-design.md)). Phase B2, the "deep" section of the same call, runs only on big skips or arc closes and only in idle time. It produces relationship and self lines and growth-ring candidates, each passing a mechanical gate: every statement cites a source, no new names or numbers, and a failure shows a visible warning chip. The prompt receives one **mind block** in words, not numbers, placed in the existing volatile tail just before `[Directive]`. It is capped at about 100 / 250 / 400 tokens by tier and omitted when the character is near baseline. Because Kataki's tail is already rebuilt each turn after the byte-stable history, the block adds about 0.1-0.25 s of GPU prefill (est.) and invalidates nothing cached.

```text
[Inside Mira right now: show it, never say it]
Feeling: hurt, and under it a little afraid; low since Aren broke his promise this morning.
Showing: cool and polite. Hiding the hurt; it slips out as short answers and not meeting his eyes.
Toward Aren: wary; you trust him less than last week. You have not forgiven that. Stay civil; do not warm up unless he owns it.
On your mind: whether the harbour job came through. Bring it up only at a natural opening; drop it if he dodges.
[Directive] Reply only as Mira. Keep it short. If he asks about the ledger, you say you gave it to Tobin. Hold your position unless he gives a new reason that matters to you.
```

The three tiers differ in calls, not in the state model:

| | LITE (CPU 3-4B, 4-6 GB GPU, or free credits) | STANDARD (8 GB default, 8-9B local) | PREMIUM (cloud, or 12 GB+ local) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affect core | Code; appraisal from rules and embedder centroids | Code; lagged side-call appraisal | Code; pre-reply mind call every turn |
| Inner thought | None (Peek shows state in words) | Inline two-line header | `thought` field of the pre-reply call |
| Honesty engine | All code | All code | Plus LLM leak verify on hits |
| Anti-sycophancy | Code; regex hits logged only | Plus one resample on a hit | Plus critic call on flagged turns |
| Memory effects | Partial cues, mood term | Plus recollections, retelling, drift | Plus recount of pressed hazy memories |
| Between job per skip | Code tick and templates, 0 calls | 1 call per tracked character; deep pass idle-only | 1-2 calls per character on the reasoning role |

Build order follows the owner's show-first method, as eight thin slices that each end in a scripted on-screen demo and guarding probes ([methodology memory](file:///C:/Users/user/.claude/projects/D--Kataki/memory/methodology-show-first.md)). "She's still upset" (affect core and mood chip, 0 new calls) comes first. Then "she holds a grudge and won't cave" (opinion ledger, side call, yield rule), "she thinks before she speaks" (inline header, measured against the Mind-graph spec's under-2-second gate), "she lies to protect a secret" (secrets, sampler, leak filter, out-of-character intercept), and "meanwhile…" (the Between job and the away card). After those come "she misremembers, and corrects herself" (recollections and the slip planner) and "she wants something" (goals and agenda). The last is "she changes, slowly", held for the owner's sign-off on the YAGNI list. Each slice is gated by a fourteen-probe set added to the existing `live_eval.py`, with mechanical pass criteria. The probes cover: bratty holds the frame, blunt won't sugarcoat, liar keeps the cover, grudge persists 30 turns, anxious waits, forgetful but honest, self-correcting, zero leaks, no caving under emotional pressure, wants something, off-screen consistency, drift at 50 turns, out-of-character honesty, and group balance. Health metrics run alongside: side-call JSON validity at or above 95%, resample rate at or below 10%, an unchanged cached-token ratio, and a gated share at or below 15%. On top sits a blind owner A/B that the slice must win on at least 60% of decided pairs. All of these thresholds are design defaults to tune after the first runs.

## An 8 GB laptop runs the standard tier

The weights, not the context, are the constraint. Qwen3.5-9B (Apache 2.0, hybrid Gated DeltaNet) is already on disk as a 5.68 GB Q4_K_M file ([Qwen3.5-9B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B); [Unsloth GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-9B-GGUF)). Only 8 of its 32 layers use full attention, so its KV cache is about **32 KiB per token**: 256 MiB at 8k and 512 MiB at 16k (arithmetic from the [config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B/raw/main/config.json)). At 8k the total is about 6.3-6.6 GB and fits the 8,151 MiB RTX 5060 Laptop. Gemma 4 12B QAT at 6.98 GB ([Google GGUF](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-12B-it-qat-q4_0-gguf)) does not fit comfortably and belongs to the 12 GB tier. The local image chain peaks at 7.03 GB, so the LLM and image generation cannot be resident together; the app must stop or sleep llama-server around image jobs ([rules-and-gotchas](file:///D:/Kataki/docs/images/rules-and-gotchas.md)). A bandwidth model anchored on the llama.cpp scoreboard, where a 4060 Ti decodes a 7B Q4 at 64 tok/s ([discussion 15013](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/15013)), predicts about **40-55 tok/s** for the 9B on this class of card. With that, the fused turn reaches its first visible word in about 1.6 s (est.). The dev machine has no scoreboard row, so this is extrapolation.

| Tier | Chat model | Expected decode (est.) | Cold 4k prefill (est.) | Cognition on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPU-only, 16 GB RAM | Qwen3.5-4B Q4 or Gemma 4 E4B | 10-20 tok/s | 20-60 s; keep context ≤4k | LITE; offstage only at skips |
| 4 GB VRAM | Qwen3.5-4B Q4, 6-8k context | 60-90 tok/s | 2-4 s | LITE plus inline thought |

| 12-16 GB VRAM | Gemma 4 12B QAT, or 24-31B RP tunes at Q3-Q4 | 35-45 (12B) | 1.5-3 s | STANDARD plus deeper Between passes, 5-6 tracked characters |
| Apple Silicon 16 GB | Gemma 4 12B QAT or Qwen3.5-9B via Metal | M4 about 13-16; M5 about 17-21 | M4 about 14 s; M5 about 4 s | STANDARD with short prompts |

The trap is caching. Both target families (hybrid Qwen3.5 and sliding-window Gemma 4) cannot use KV shifting or `--cache-reuse`, because a slid window "forgets" old KV ([PR 13194](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/13194)). They depend on context checkpoints, which have open bugs: issue 24055 reports checkpoints "always invalidated on hybrid/recurrent models" ([issue 24055](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/24055)). The default `--checkpoint-min-step` of 8192 means roughly one checkpoint per 8k chat. The recommended config is one GPU server with thinking off, `--checkpoint-min-step 256` (verify it), a 4 GB host-RAM prompt cache, sleep after 10 idle minutes, per-request JSON schema, and no context shift. Its first measurement should be whether turn 2 logs "forcing full prompt re-processing". If it does, the fallback is to freeze each turn's volatile block into history. Grammar constraints guarantee syntax, not semantics, and stricter formats can cost reasoning quality ([arXiv 2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442)). This is why the fused schema keeps a short free-text thought and a flat object. MTP speculative decoding was merged in May 2026 ([PR 22673](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22673)) and claims 1.5-2x ([Unsloth MTP card](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-9B-MTP-GGUF)), but it forbids a second slot and should gain less at roleplay temperatures, so it is an A/B, not a default. KoboldCpp is the single-exe fallback runtime that bundles image, STT and TTS ([KoboldCpp wiki](https://github.com/LostRuins/koboldcpp/wiki)). Ten local measurements settle the design, cache hits and divergence recovery first. None has been run, because the project rule is never to start the user's llama-server.

## Cheap cloud costs cents; hosted voice is the budget-breaker

Cloud prices collapsed. A usable roleplay reply model costs about **$0.15 in / $0.50 out per million tokens**: GLM-5.3-Flash, with cached input at $0.03 ([Z.ai](https://docs.z.ai/guides/overview/pricing)). A utility JSON model costs about $0.03 / $0.13 (Qwen3.7-Flash, [Alibaba](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/model-pricing)). DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is half price off-peak, and a cache hit costs 2% of a miss ([DeepSeek](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing)). Gemini 3.x Flash prices are promotional and double after 31 December 2026 ([Google](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). The same model varies up to 8x across hosts, and structured-output support differs per host ([HF router catalogue](https://router.huggingface.co/v1/models)), so a Kataki router should choose per model on price, JSON support and latency. Hugging Face passes provider prices through with no markup and gives free accounts $0.10 of monthly credit and PRO accounts $2 ([HF billing](https://huggingface.co/docs/inference-providers/pricing)). The cost model assumes 3k in / 300 out per reply with 70% prefix-cache hits, which is why Kataki's append-only prompt layout matters for the bill as well as for latency.

Arch B is the reply plus a side call on 40% of turns and extraction on 20%; all figures come from note 21's arithmetic on the cited list prices ([OpenRouter catalogue](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models); [HF router catalogue](https://router.huggingface.co/v1/models)). Three conclusions follow. The "human-like mind" extras add only about 10% to the bill when they run on a nano-class model; the reply model's price decides the bill. "Speak locally, think in the cloud" is the cheapest way to sell premium cognition, at $0.22-4.46 a month. And quality-tier models cost 8-20x more, so they must be sold as credits, not as an unlimited plan. Voice and images, not text, break budgets. Hosted TTS runs $0.0036-0.072 per spoken minute ([ElevenLabs API](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api); [Together](https://www.together.ai/pricing)), so a light user voicing every reply costs about $24 a month at $15 per million characters, roughly 40x the text bill. Images cost $0.003-0.06 each ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/pricing); [Together](https://www.together.ai/pricing)). Voice therefore stays local by default and images sit in a quota.

These economics support three packages, which note 21 recommends combining. **"Open and Yours"** is the free local app with BYOK and local models: no meter, and the app never stops working. It adds an optional ~$25 supporter licence (Obsidian's Catalyst precedent ([Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/pricing))) and optional $4 sync. The supporter licence keeps about 93% after Paddle's 5% + $0.50 fee ([Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). **"Kataki Plus"** is $7.99 a month (about $69 a year) for sync and the cheap hosted stack, capped at about 5,000 replies a month behind a visible counter. Past the cap it offers credits or BYOK, never a lock-out of local mode. Margin is about 78% for a typical user, 63% at the cap with caching and 47% without; through Apple's 30% cut it falls to 44% at the cap. **"Credits"** are $10 packs at 1.5x provider cost that never expire, for quality models, hosted voice and images, at about 23% margin. For comparison, SillyTavern is free and AGPL ([GitHub](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern)), NovelAI's top tier is $25 ([NovelAI](https://docs.novelai.net/en/subscription)), and Featherless sells flat $25 roleplay inference ([Featherless](https://featherless.ai/pricing)). At $7.99, Kataki undercuts every paid companion while keeping the local app whole. Two structural constraints apply. Stripe prohibits AI-generated adult content ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)), and Apple requires in-app purchase and content moderation ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). So the hosted tier should be SFW by default, with adult content local or BYOK only. These are unit economics: they exclude free-tier subsidy, support, refunds and the real distribution of turns, cache hits and regenerations, which only engine telemetry can supply.

## Decisions only the owner can make

The research settles the architecture but leaves several choices that belong to the owner or to counsel. The spec's YAGNI list collides with parts of the design ([M0/M1 spec](file:///D:/Kataki/docs/specs/2026-09-18-m0-m1-design.md)). "Mutating stored memories to fake distortion" is compatible as designed, because truth rows are never touched and drift lives in per-knower recollection rows, but the line's wording should be confirmed. "Reflection / dream cycles" is directly contradicted by the deep Between pass and growth rings, and needs explicit sign-off before slice 8. "Summary roll-ups", "higher-order theory of mind" and "per-turn extraction" are handled by design: reflections regenerate from raw sources, only one second-order slot exists, and it is PREMIUM-only; the side call writes mind state, not memories.

The honest caveats are substantial. Almost every latency, token, threshold, half-life and margin figure in this report is an estimate; none was measured on the RTX 5060 Laptop. The strongest human-rated studies (Inner Thoughts, Chain-of-Emotion, ReCon, the Turing test) used GPT-class models, and no source tests thought headers, appraisal, lying or closed-enum event labelling on 7-14B local models. Much of the 2026 literature was read at abstract level. Market prices come from aggregators that conflict, the SB 243 variable-reward question is unresolved between sources ([SonderMind](https://www.sondermind.com/resources/articles-and-content/california-sb-243-sets-a-new-regulatory-baseline-for-ai-companion-chatbots/) versus [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/understanding-the-new-wave-of-chatbot-legislation-california-sb-243-and-beyond/)), and Front Porch AI's features are self-described. Several mechanisms are design with no evidence behind them: jealousy, gossip distortion, boredom as a topic driver, and whether surfaced background thoughts feel human. The probe set and blind A/Bs are the only way to turn these bets into knowledge.

## Conclusion

The central finding changes what "more human" means for Kataki. The industry treats human-likeness as a model-quality problem and answers it with bigger models and longer reasoning. The evidence says reasoning hurts roleplay and alignment is the obstacle, and it locates most of the value in small, persistent, rule-driven state: who knows what, who has not forgiven whom, what she is hiding and why, what she has half-forgotten, what happened while you were gone. That state is cheap, deterministic, testable and inspectable. Kataki's existing engine (per-knower epistemic memory, story-time decay, one-speaker generation, the Peek and Backstage views) is already the hard half of it, which is why the whole design adds only one small call per turn. The same code-owned state is also what makes lying characters legally and ethically defensible: the truth is always one tap away for the user, even when a character hides it from another.

The strategic implication is that Kataki's moat is not a model but an inspectable mind, which runs well on the 8 GB laptop the market left behind. It can therefore be priced like software rather than like compute: a free local app, a sub-$8 hosted convenience tier and credits for luxuries. The next step is not more research but measurement. Run the cache-hit test and the fused-schema test on the current 9B, ship "she's still upset" and "she holds a grudge", and let the probe set decide whether the rest of the design earns its place.
